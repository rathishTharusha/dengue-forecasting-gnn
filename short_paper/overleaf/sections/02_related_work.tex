\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\cE{\textbf{ST-GNNs for epidemic forecasting.} Spatio-temporal graph networks
couple a graph convolution over regions with a temporal operator over history.
The architectures we evaluate---STGAT, A3TGCN, ASTGCN, DCRNN and
AAGCN---originate in traffic forecasting and were transferred to dengue by Weng
et al.~\cite{weng2024graph}, who report them on the dataset used here.
GulMohamed et al.~\cite{gulmohamed2026denguegnn} propose per-horizon output
layers, additive temporal attention and a Gaussian predictive head; we adopt all
three as increments and test them (\S\ref{sec:results}).}

\cF{\textbf{Mechanistic models and hybrids.} Compartmental models remain the
standard description of dengue transmission; we use the SEIR--SEI formulation and
sensitivity analysis of Phaijoo and Gurung~\cite{phaijoo2018sensitivity}, whose
nine published sensitivity indices we reproduce. Physics-informed neural networks
embed such dynamics as a loss term~\cite{raissi2019pinn}, and recent work applies
them to epidemic curves, typically in a regime where data are scarce relative to
the dynamics---MP-PINN~\cite{nguyen2024mppinn} trains on 35 days and forecasts 97.
That is the regime where a mechanistic prior pays. Our setting is the opposite:
459 weeks across 25 districts forecasting three weeks, where a naive baseline is
already near-optimal. We show that the difference is not incidental but decides
the outcome, and we quantify the crossing point in terms of the predictability of
the reproduction number.}
