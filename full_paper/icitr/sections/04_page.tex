\section{The \model{} Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
Let $\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E},\mathbf{A})$ be the district graph with
$|\mathcal{V}|=N$ and adjacency $\mathbf{A}\in\{0,1\}^{N\times N}$, and let
$y_{i,t}\ge0$ be the cases reported by district $i$ in week $t$. We model the
standardised log count $z_{i,t}=(\log(1+y_{i,t})-m)/s$, with $m$ and $s$ computed on
training weeks only. At forecast origin $t$ the input is the window
$\mathbf{Z}_t=[z_{i,t-W+1},\dots,z_{i,t}]_{i\in\mathcal{V}}\in\R^{N\times W}$ and,
optionally, covariates $\mathbf{E}_t\in\R^{N\times F}$ at causal lags. The task is to
predict $y_{i,t+h}$ for every district and horizon $h=1,\dots,H$.

\subsection{Encoder}
Any spatio-temporal encoder $f_\theta$ maps the window to one representation per
district, $\mathbf{H}=f_\theta(\mathbf{Z}_t,\mathbf{A})\in\R^{N\times d}$, and
covariates are concatenated around it, $\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_i=[\mathbf{h}_i\Vert
\mathbf{e}_{i,t}]$, so that a published architecture runs exactly as designed. A
linear read-out gives $\mathbf{u}_i=\mathbf{W}\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_i+\mathbf{b}\in\R^{H}$.
Published models output $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_i=\mathbf{u}_i$; we call this the
\emph{direct} head.

\subsection{Persistence-Anchored Gated Head}
\label{sec:page}
\model{} anchors every horizon on the last observed week, $p_i=z_{i,t}$, and lets the
network supply only a gated departure from it (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}):
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{\delta}_i=\mathbf{u}_i-p_i\mathbf{1}_H,\qquad
  \hat{\mathbf{z}}_i=p_i\mathbf{1}_H+g\,\boldsymbol{\delta}_i,\qquad g=\sigma(a),
  \label{eq:page}
\end{equation}
where $a$ is one learned scalar initialised at $a_0<0$, so training starts near the
persistence forecast. Equivalently, $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_i=(1-g)\,p_i\mathbf{1}_H+
g\,\mathbf{u}_i$ is a learned convex combination of persistence and the encoder's
forecast. Counts are recovered as
$\hat y_{i,t+h}=\max\!\big(0,\exp(s\hat z_{i,h}+m)-1\big)$.

Three properties follow. (i)~\emph{The identity is free}: at $g\to0$ the model is
exactly persistence, which a direct head must instead carry through its message
passing and recurrence. (ii)~\emph{The gate is a learned shrinkage}: $g$ grows only as
far as $\mathbf{u}_i$ improves on $p_i$, so a weak encoder cannot pull the forecast far
from a strong baseline. (iii)~\emph{It costs one parameter} and no extra computation.
Setting $g\equiv1$ with an unconstrained departure gives the \emph{residual} head, and
removing the anchor gives the direct head; both are ablations.

\subsection{Optional SEIR Branch}
\label{sec:seir}
To test whether transmission dynamics help, the read-out can be replaced by a
differentiable SEIR model. The encoder predicts a force of infection
$\lambda_{i,h}=\min(\exp(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_i+b_\lambda),\lambda_{\max})$,
and $\dot S=-\lambda S$, $\dot E=\lambda S-\omega E$, $\dot I=\omega E-\gamma I$,
$\dot R=\gamma I$ are stepped daily from a state $\mathbf{s}_{i,t}$ seeded by the
observed cases of weeks $t-1$ and $t$ divided by a reporting rate $\rho$. The weekly
flow $E\to I$, scaled by $\rho$ and the district population, gives counts whose
standardised log replaces $\mathbf{u}_i$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:page}. With the anchor and
gate this is the SEIR-GNN (SEIR-LSTM when $f_\theta$ is an LSTM); without them it is a
pure SEIR decoder.

\subsection{Learning Objective}
All parameters $\Theta=\{\theta,\mathbf{W},\mathbf{b},a\}$, plus $\mathbf{w}$,
$b_\lambda$ and learned scales on the seeded state and $\rho$ when the SEIR branch is
used, minimise the squared error on the standardised scale over a mini-batch
$\mathcal{B}$ of origins,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\Theta)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|NH}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{B}}
  \sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\big(\hat z^{(t)}_{i,h}-z_{i,t+h}\big)^2,
\end{equation}
with early stopping on validation RMSE in counts. We also test a negative-binomial
(NB) likelihood, because $\exp$ of a squared-error fit on $\log(1+y)$ estimates a
conditional median while RMSE rewards the conditional mean.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:impl}
$N=25$, $W=3$, $H=3$, $d=64$, $a_0=-2$ ($g_0=0.12$). SEIR rates are per day:
$\omega=0.1$, $\gamma=1/7$, $\rho=1/11$, $\lambda_{\max}=1/7$, with stage durations
from~\cite{phaijoo2018sensitivity} and an initial susceptible fraction of 0.318 from a
2013--14 Colombo serosurvey~\cite{jeewandara2015seroprevalence}. Training uses Adam (learning rate $3\times10^{-3}$ with cosine decay,
weight decay $10^{-4}$), batch size 32, dropout 0.1, at most 300 epochs (400 for NB), early stopping
with patience 40, and the best-validation weights restored; seeds 0, 1, 2. All models
are written in plain PyTorch with dense $N\times N$ graph operations, and each
published encoder is checked against its reference implementation in unit tests.
The main study (810 runs) ran in 3.0 hours on a 4-core Kaggle CPU; the prospective study
ran on a laptop CPU (Intel Core i5-12450H). No GPU is needed.
