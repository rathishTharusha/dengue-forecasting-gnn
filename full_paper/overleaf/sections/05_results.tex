\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All numbers are RMSE in weekly cases on the corrected dataset. Validation
decides and test is reported beside it. Unless stated otherwise, pairs are on
the (origin, seed) unit of the frozen three origins.

\subsection{Six encoders, one harness}
\label{sec:encoders}
Table~\ref{tab:encoders} and Figure~\ref{fig:encoders} give the comparison the
SEIR-GNN question needs, with every encoder sharing folds, loss, early stopping
and metric.

\textbf{Architecture matters little, and the graph less.} Behind the direct head
AAGCN, ASTGCN and the LSTM score 16.76, 16.84 and 16.91: a spread of 0.15, and
all three beat persistence (17.86). The LSTM has no message passing at all.
A GCN beats the same network without message passing by only 0.07 (4/9,
Table~\ref{tab:levers}). STGAT, A3TGCN and DCRNN score 23.32, 28.63 and 34.89, all worse than
persistence; STGAT and A3TGCN are worse at every horizon. A linear model on the same inputs
recovers 47--62\% of STGAT's and A3TGCN's residual variance out of sample but
only 4--6\% of the working encoders'. The failing encoders are underfitting,
limited by their wiring rather than by the data.

\textbf{The SEIR decoder fails on every encoder.} Routing the forecast entirely
through the simulator scores 23.69--25.72: 6.9--7.4 worse than the direct head
on each working encoder. The deficit is structural, and we measured it without
training by inverting the simulator for the force of infection that reproduces
each observed count:
(i) with transmission switched off, the $E$ already seeded from recent cases
still emits \emph{more} cases than observed for 14--16\% of targets, which are
therefore unreachable at any network output;
(ii) the required force of infection is only $r^2=0.22$--$0.26$ predictable from
case history, against 0.81--0.82 for the direct target;
(iii) it is not an optimisation problem, since ten times the training budget
bought 0.04;
(iv) it is not susceptible depletion, since only 2.5\% of cells reach the
susceptible clamp.
Point (ii) replicates on the corrected data, through a different mechanism,
our earlier renewal-equation finding on the benchmark array: the reproduction
number is about 26\% predictable out of sample.

\textbf{The gated SEIR head repairs the failing encoders.} Letting the
simulator supply only the departure from persistence brings all six encoders to
17.44--17.69, within 0.25 of each other and below persistence on validation
(17.86) and on test (35.50--35.84 against 36.02). For the three failing encoders
this is a large repair, on 9/9 paired units each:
STGAT $-5.70$ (test $-24.99$), A3TGCN $-10.95$ ($-24.90$) and DCRNN $-17.22$
($-38.23$). It costs the working encoders 0.63--0.78 on validation. Read at face
value, this is the physics-informed result the study set out to find. The next
section shows that it is not.

\subsection{Anchor, gate or physics?}
\label{sec:rescue}
The gated SEIR head differs from the direct head in three ways at once: it
forecasts a departure from last week's value (the anchor), it shrinks that
departure through a learned gate, and the departure comes from the SEIR
simulator. Only the third is physics. A pre-registered control separates them
by rerunning all six encoders behind four heads that add these one at a time:
direct, residual (anchor), gated (anchor and gate) and gated SEIR (all three).
The rescue is credited to physics only if gated SEIR beats its no-physics twin on
at least 7 of 9 units with $\padj<0.05$ on each failing encoder. Our stated
prediction was that the anchor and gate carry most of the rescue.

Table~\ref{tab:rescue} gives the result: \textbf{the rescue is the anchor, not
the physics}. Anchor and gate alone account for 96--112\% of the validation repair
on each failing encoder. A plain residual head, with no gate and no simulator,
already brings them to 17.47--17.84. Adding the SEIR simulator to the gated
head earns credit on none of the three. It is significantly \emph{worse} on
STGAT ($+0.67$, 2/9, $\padj=0.047$), ties on A3TGCN ($-0.00$, 6/9), and helps
DCRNN by $-0.68$ on only 3/9 units ($\padj=0.38$). On the three working
encoders the simulator costs 0.74--0.88 on 0/9 units each. The best head for
AAGCN on this grid is the gated head without physics (16.58 against the direct
head's 16.76; $-0.18$, 8/9), a comparison we did not pre-register and report
only as a lead. Our prediction was right that anchor and gate carry the repair
(share $>0.8$). It was wrong that the simulator's effect would stay within
$\pm0.3$, and wrong that the residual head would rescue less than the gated one.

On test the picture reverses. The SEIR head beats its no-physics twin on the
failing encoders (A3TGCN $-1.65$ and DCRNN $-3.33$, both 9/9; STGAT $-0.83$,
7/9). That is the recurring disagreement of \S\ref{sec:valtest}. It was not used
to decide anything, and we do not claim it.

\subsection{What moves the metric}
\label{sec:levers}
Table~\ref{tab:levers} pairs every lever we tried against its own control, and
Figure~\ref{fig:levers} shows the same deltas. Two levers improve validation
consistently. The \textbf{negative-binomial likelihood} is the largest controlled
gain: $-0.41$, 9/9, $\padj=0.016$, larger than any architecture difference. It
corrects a bias, since squared error on $\log(1+y)$ targets a median while RMSE
scores a mean.
\textbf{Seasonal features} give the largest mean change ($-0.81$) but win only
6/9. Combined, the best model $B$ (AAGCN, direct head, NB, seasonal features)
scores 15.66 against persistence's 17.86: $-2.20$ on 9/9 units.

That is the headline a benchmark would report. It does not hold out of
sample: on test, $B$ scores 37.55 against persistence's 36.02, and the NB
likelihood is \emph{worse} on test by 1.57. We return to this in
\S\ref{sec:valtest}.

\subsection{SEIR-GNN against SEIR-LSTM}
\label{sec:seirgnn}
The comparison the research question poses is a graph encoder against the LSTM,
behind the same gated SEIR head. On three origins the graph wins on validation
every time: ASTGCN $-0.62$ (9/9), AAGCN $-0.18$ (9/9) with NB and seasonal
features, and AAGCN $-0.17$ (9/9) in a separate grid. Behind the direct head the
same graph gains nothing ($-0.14$, 5/9). So what the graph adds appeared
specific to estimating a transmission quantity, where spatial coupling is
mechanistically plausible.

The nine-origin confirmation does not support that reading
(Table~\ref{tab:nine}). In the first run, the graph's advantage holds on validation
($-0.75$, 8/9, $p=0.012$) and \emph{reverses} on test ($+0.23$, 4/9). In the
second, with a different encoder, it vanishes on validation ($-0.01$) and
appears on test ($-0.41$, 6/9, $p=0.031$). Neither run clears its pre-registered
corrected threshold. The two confirmations also named different endpoints
(validation for the first, test for the second), a flaw in our own
pre-registration that we record rather than resolve after the fact. We
therefore do not claim that the SEIR-GNN beats the SEIR-LSTM. On three origins
it does so consistently on validation but not on test, where ASTGCN's gated
SEIR head is $+0.30$ against the SEIR-LSTM. On nine origins it is not robust to
the encoder, the metric or the run.

\subsection{Physics as structure: penalty and metapopulation coupling}
\label{sec:physicsstruct}
Two further physics-informed structures were pre-registered against $B$. The
\textbf{spatial physics penalty} was the one significant physics gain in our
earlier work on the benchmark array ($-0.044$ on STGAT, 57/60 runs). On $B$ it
keeps its sign ($-0.06$, and $-0.20$ at horizon 3) but wins only 6/9 and is not
adopted. The \textbf{metapopulation SEIR head}, which couples districts inside
the dynamics rather than through the initial state, is \emph{worse} than the
gated head ($+0.20$, 2/9). On nine origins it ties the SEIR-LSTM ($-0.01$ on
test, 4/9). The reason is in the data. On the benchmark array, Moran's $I$ of
$\log R$ on the district graph is 0.005, against 0.274 for log cases.
Transmission has almost no local spatial structure at district-week
resolution, so a learned coupling has nothing to encode.

\subsection{The ceiling is informational}
\label{sec:ceiling}
Table~\ref{tab:levers} records what did not work. Architecture changes (district
seasonal curves, a nonlinear head, a global context node, a learned adjacency)
move validation by less than 0.15. The literature's remedies for weak
spatio-temporal models are neutral or worse. RevIN costs 1.27 (0/9), and
$k$-nearest-neighbour analogues with no network cost 0.87. Data-side levers fail
too. Halving the training windows costs 0.22, and the learning curve is flat past
75\%. TimeGAN augmentation costs 0.53 (1/9) and truncates the tail: its largest
generated week is 882 cases against a real 2,631. Climate at lags 2--13 costs
0.09--0.13. Gradient-boosted trees gain from climate ($-0.49$), which a network
does only once its head is nonlinear, and then the nonlinear head costs as much
as climate gains.

These failures share a cause. The residuals of every model family we trained
correlate 0.87--0.99 with $B$'s, including $k$-NN, which has no network and no
training, so the models make the same errors. The remaining error is a lag on
moves. Models under-predict rising weeks by 11--13 cases and over-predict falling
weeks by 10--11, and the top 5\% of district-weeks carry $\sim$60\% of the squared
error. That is what a mean-seeking objective produces on a near-random-walk
target whose next move is not in the inputs.

\subsection{Validation and test disagree, systematically}
\label{sec:valtest}
In five separate grids, models anchored to the last observation score better
on test than the direct model that validation selects, and validation reverses
the order each time. Examples: the residual head with NB (34.55 against $B$'s
37.55), $k$-NN (34.34), the gated SEIR arms (34.92--35.34), and on nine origins
the gated SEIR-GNN at 31.06 against $B$ at 33.21 and persistence at 31.76.
Nothing here was selected on test, and none of these differences is significant
on nine origins (the gated SEIR-GNN against $B$ is $-2.15$ on test, 6/9,
$p=0.20$). Five grids agreeing is nonetheless evidence about the selection
protocol. A 30-week validation span immediately before each origin appears to
reward fitting that span's level, which does not carry into the next period.
Acting on it needs a new pre-registered protocol and a test set nothing here has
touched, which only data after March 2024 can provide.
