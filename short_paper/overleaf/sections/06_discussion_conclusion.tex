\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\cB{\textbf{When does a mechanistic prior pay?} PINNs succeed where data are
scarce relative to the dynamics---MP-PINN~\cite{nguyen2024mppinn} trains on 35
days and forecasts 97. Ours is the opposite regime, and the decisive quantity is
not sample size but how much a naive baseline already explains: here $r^2=0.85$,
leaving an $R_t$ that is 26\% predictable. That ratio predicts \emph{which} use
survives---a prior supplying a forecast inherits $R_t$'s error, one restricting the
hypothesis space does not.}

\cC{\textbf{Reporting.} A caution that applies to us as much as to the work we
reproduce: at $n=9$ the spatial constraint ($p=0.057$) and the growth envelope
($p=0.238$) were indistinguishable; at $n\ge60$ one holds and the other is a coin
flip. Small effects on folds this noisy need the seeds to separate them.}

\cD{\textbf{Limitations.} One country, one disease, one processed array. We do not
evaluate GAN augmentation---the remaining proposed contribution---and the detection
result uses a single-week threshold where sustained exceedance would be more
operationally meaningful.}

\cE{\textbf{Conclusion.} Under a leak-free, artifact-aware protocol no reproduced
architecture and no increment separates from a persistence floor of 29.52, and
reconstructing a forecast through the mechanism cannot close that gap. But the
mechanism holds in the two roles that do not ask it to predict---as an inequality
constraint and as a state estimate for ranking outbreaks. Knowing \emph{when} is
where the mathematics comes first. Code and a reproduction notebook accompany this
paper.}
