\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Dengue is the fastest-spreading mosquito-borne viral disease, and Sri Lanka
faces recurring severe epidemics: the 2017 outbreak alone recorded over 186{,}000
cases and 440 deaths~\cite{tissera2020severe}. Roughly three weeks separate
infection from diagnosis. District-level forecasts issued $H=3$ weeks ahead
therefore carry actionable lead time for clinical capacity and vector control.

Two families of model are proposed for this task. Spatio-temporal graph neural
networks, transferred from traffic forecasting, treat districts as nodes and
learn from neighbours; Weng et al.~\cite{weng2024graph} benchmark five of them
on Sri Lanka's record. SEIR-informed models push the forecast through
compartmental dynamics; Liu et al.~\cite{liu2025seirlstm} drive an SEIR model
with an LSTM on the same country. The natural hybrid is an \emph{SEIR-GNN}:
the SEIR-LSTM with its LSTM replaced by a graph encoder, so that spatial
coupling and mechanism act together. Whether that hybrid helps, and through
which component, is the question of this paper.

One property of the data governs every answer: weekly incidence is strongly
autocorrelated. Cases at $t-1$ explain $r^2=0.85$ of cases at $t$, while the
best single climate covariate explains $0.02$. Every model competes with the
naive persistence forecast $\hat{y}_{t+h}=y_t$. A benchmark that omits it
cannot tell whether a model contributed anything, and the reference benchmark
omits it.

Our first SEIR-GNN pipeline reported a win, and it was wrong. It trained for
about sixty gradient steps, optimised SMAPE while scoring RMSE, held the force
of infection constant across the horizon, and collapsed every covariate to a
scalar before the graph saw it. This paper reports the controlled re-run. Its
protocol and each experiment's decision rule were committed before the runs,
and negative results are reported as such.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
  \item \textbf{A data audit and a corrected dataset} (\S\ref{sec:data}). The
        benchmark array mixes time orders, puts post-test data in every
        training split, and feeds future climate as ``lagged'' features. We
        rebuild 559 weeks from source reports, with causal lags enforced by
        perturbation tests.
  \item \textbf{One harness for six encoders and four SEIR-informed heads}
        (\S\ref{sec:models}), with validation-only selection, paired tests at
        two units, and a nine-origin confirmation.
  \item \textbf{Whether physics helps} (\S\ref{sec:results}). A pure SEIR
        decoder fails on every encoder, for reasons we measure. A gated SEIR head
        appears to repair the three encoders that fail as published, but a
        pre-registered control attributes 96--112\% of that repair to the
        persistence anchor and none of it to the simulator
        (\S\ref{sec:rescue}).
  \item \textbf{An informational ceiling, measured} (\S\ref{sec:ceiling}).
        More than twenty architectural, data and physics levers leave the same
        errors behind (residual correlation 0.87--0.99).
\end{itemize}
