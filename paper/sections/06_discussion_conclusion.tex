\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Discussion}
We set out to improve dengue forecasting by learning district connectivity and
constraining forecasts spatially. One worked, narrowly; the other did not; and
neither closed the gap to a naive baseline.

The learned adjacency is worth keeping: against a control sharing its propagation
path it improves RMSE, and the gate settles part-way between geography and the
learned graph rather than collapsing to either, indicating both carry signal. But
the improvement is small against a fold-to-fold spread an order of magnitude
larger, and nine paired runs cannot resolve it.

The regulariser's failure is instructive. A smoothness penalty is minimised by a
more uniform graph, so it pulls the gate back toward geography and works against
the adaptive component. Whatever suppresses spurious spatial spikes here, an
undirected smoothness prior over the same adjacency the model is learning is
not it.

The peak-timing result is what we would carry forward. Both graph models call the
peak two to three weeks later than persistence, and the better model by RMSE is
the worse by timing. For a system meant to give warning before a peak, that
trade-off runs the wrong way, and it is invisible in aggregate error.

\subsection{Limitations}
\textbf{(1) Power.} Nine paired runs reject a large effect but not a half-point
one; more origins is the cheapest remedy.
\textbf{(2) Scale.} $N=25$ districts; whether a learned graph behaves differently
at sub-district resolution is untested.
\textbf{(3) Point forecasts only}, so calibration is unassessed.
\textbf{(4) Superseded figures.} Results here come from a rebuilt implementation;
earlier numbers produced by code that was not retained should not be cited.

\subsection{Future Work}
A soft SEIR--SEI residual remains untested and is the natural successor to the
smoothness penalty that failed here, constraining dynamics rather than spatial
uniformity so that it does not fight the learned graph. Given that timing and
magnitude diverge, a loss weighting outbreak onset directly is more promising
than further RMSE reduction. Beyond that: conditional time-series generative
models~\cite{yoon2019timegan} for outbreak tails, judged on downstream accuracy
rather than distributional similarity; probabilistic forecasts with CRPS and
interval coverage~\cite{bracher2021evaluating}; and multi-country validation on
OpenDengue~\cite{clarke2024opendengue}.
