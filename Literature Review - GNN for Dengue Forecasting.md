# Literature Review: Physics-Informed, GAN-Augmented Graph Neural Networks for Dengue Incidence Forecasting

## TL;DR
- A spatio-temporal GNN backbone for dengue forecasting is well-justified by recent work (an STGCN adapted from traffic prediction that forecast weekly dengue across nine South/Central American countries with R² of 0.78–0.98 in short-term horizons; DengueGNN; and the Weng et al. 2024 NASA/IEEE BigData paper using the *exact* Sri Lanka 25-district 2013–2022 dataset), and epidemic-mechanistic loss functions (EINN, CausalGNN, HeatGNN, DINN) and GAN time-series augmentation (TimeGAN, RCGAN, DoppelGANger) are each independently validated — but **no published work combines all three (spatio-temporal GNN + epidemic-physics-informed loss + GAN augmentation) for dengue or any other domain**, which is the defensible novelty gap.
- The strongest justifications are: for the GNN choice — EpiGNN, ColaGNN, Graph WaveNet, and the Sri Lanka-specific Weng et al. (2024); for the physics-informed loss — EINN (Rodríguez et al., AAAI 2023), CausalGNN (Wang et al., AAAI 2022), and the dengue SEIR-SEI (host-vector) mechanistic family; for GAN augmentation — TimeGAN (Yoon et al., NeurIPS 2019) and PID-GAN (Daw et al., KDD 2021).
- Recommended positioning: baseline GCN/GAT → add an SEIR-SEI (host-vector) residual + population-conservation soft-constraint loss → add TimeGAN/conditional-GAN augmentation, with an ablation that isolates each component, evaluated with rolling-origin cross-validation and both point (RMSE/MAE/MAPE) and probabilistic (CRPS, PICP/MPIW) metrics plus Moran's I for spatial structure.

## Key Findings

**The exact prior art exists and must be cited as the baseline.** Weng et al., "Graph Representation Learning for Dengue Forecasting" (NASA Technical Reports Server / IEEE BigData 2024) uses the identical Sri Lanka 25-district weekly dataset (2013–2022), NASA satellite meteorological covariates spatially joined to GADM district shapefiles, GIS-polygon nodes, a 3-week window and 3-step-ahead forecasting (a window justified by consulting a Sri Lanka epidemiologist that infection-to-diagnosis averages ~3 weeks). Critically, the authors state their paper "seeks not to introduce novel methods for disease forecasting, but evaluates" existing spatio-temporal GNNs against traditional early-warning systems such as ARIMA — explicitly leaving architecture, loss, and augmentation innovations open. This is simultaneously the closest baseline and the clearest statement of the gap your project fills.

**Each of the three pillars is individually well-supported in the 2020–2026 literature; the triple combination is not.** An independent targeted search confirmed no paper combines all three techniques in any domain; the nearest neighbors are GNN+physics (TG-PhyNN, HeatGNN, CausalGNN, EARTH) and GNN+GAN (traffic-imputation GANs such as Di-GraphGAN). This is the core novelty claim.

## Details

### Area 1 — GNN / spatio-temporal graph methods for dengue and epidemic forecasting

**Dengue/epidemic-specific GNN papers (strongest for justifying the GNN choice):**
- **Weng et al. (2024), "Graph Representation Learning for Dengue Forecasting."** NASA Technical Reports Server / IEEE BigData 2024. Uses the exact Sri Lanka 25-district dataset; demonstrates spatio-temporal GNNs consistently outperform traditional early-warning systems (ARIMA) on MAE and RMSE. **The direct baseline** — and the paper that explicitly defers methodological novelty.
- **Siabi, Son, Thomas, Irrgang & Saynisch-Wagner (2026), "Innovative application of a traffic-prediction spatio-temporal graph convolutional network for dengue disease forecasting."** *Scientific Reports* 16:2594, DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-36225-7 (published 2026-01-17). Adapts a traffic-origin STGCN to weekly dengue in nine South/Central American countries 2014–2022, reporting R² of 0.78–0.98 and relative RMSE of 0.14–0.43 in short-term forecasts, and that "STGCN outperformed the baseline random forest model, especially in short-term forecasts" (1–2 weeks). Strong recent evidence that traffic-lineage STGCNs transfer to dengue.
- **"DengueGNN: Graph-based deep learning for modeling disease spread dynamics and prediction."** *Scientific Reports*, DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-43073-y. Dynamic ST-GNN with region nodes, human-mobility/adjacency edges, graph-convolution layers for spatial dependence + attention-augmented LSTMs for temporal dynamics; emphasizes multi-horizon, uncertainty-aware forecasts.
- **Xie, Zhang, Li, Zhou & Tan (2022), EpiGNN**, "Exploring Spatial Transmission with Graph Neural Network for Regional Epidemic Forecasting," ECML-PKDD 2022, DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-26422-1_29 (arXiv:2208.11517). Transmission-risk encoding module + Region-Aware Graph Learner; on five real-world epidemic datasets it outperforms SOTA baselines by 9.48% in RMSE overall (specifically 5.6% lower RMSE on influenza and 13.4% lower on COVID-19 than the best baselines). Can incorporate external human-mobility resources.
- **Deng, Wang, Rangwala, Wang & Ning (2020), ColaGNN**, "Cross-location Attention based Graph Neural Networks for Long-term ILI Prediction," CIKM 2020, DOI 10.1145/3340531.3411975. Cross-location attention dynamically captures dependencies between non-adjacent regions; strong for long-horizon forecasting and data efficiency.
- **Qiu, Tan & Bao (2024), MSGNN**, "Multi-scale Spatio-temporal Graph Neural Network for epidemic forecasting," *Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery* 38:2348–2376, DOI 10.1007/s10618-024-01035-w. Graph-learning module captures long-range trans-regional epidemic signals in a multi-scale graph.
- **HybridGNN / EpiHybridGNN (2025)**, arXiv:2511.15469 — fuses ColaGNN's cross-location attention with EpiGNN's risk-encoding/RAGL for balanced short- and long-term forecasting.

**Foundational / SOTA architectures (justify baseline and comparators):**
- **Kipf & Welling (2017), GCN**, "Semi-Supervised Classification with Graph Convolutional Networks," ICLR 2017, arXiv:1609.02907. Foundational spectral-approximation graph convolution; the natural GCN baseline.
- **Veličković et al. (2018), GAT** — graph attention networks; the natural GAT baseline.
- **Wu et al. (2019), Graph WaveNet**, IJCAI 2019, DOI 10.5555/3367243.3367303 — self-adaptive/learned adjacency matrix (`A_adaptive = g(E_A · E_Aᵀ)`) + dilated causal convolutions; the key reference for **learned/adaptive graph construction**.
- **Guo et al. (2019), ASTGCN** (attention-based ST-GCN); **Li, Yu, Shahabi & Liu (2018), DCRNN** (diffusion convolutional recurrent NN, ICLR 2018); **STGCN, STGAT, A3TGCN, AAGCN** — the traffic-forecasting lineage repeatedly adapted to epidemics.

**Graph construction for dengue:** geographic adjacency / district-distance (Weng et al. built their adjacency organically from inter-district distances because of a lack of travel data); human-mobility/gravity edges (DengueGNN); correlation-based; and learned/adaptive adjacency (Graph WaveNet's node-embedding product). For only 25 districts, a learned/adaptive adjacency is computationally feasible and is itself a defensible novelty axis relative to Weng et al.'s fixed distance matrix.

**Known GNN limitations for dengue:** (1) **data scarcity / small node count** (25 districts × ~520 weeks) — the primary motivation for both the physics-informed loss and GAN augmentation; (2) **over-smoothing** — Li, Han & Wu (2018), "Deeper Insights into GCNs for Semi-Supervised Learning," showed graph convolution is a special form of Laplacian smoothing that drives node embeddings to indistinguishable vectors at depth, so keep the GNN shallow (2–3 layers) or use residual / JumpingKnowledge / DropEdge remedies; (3) **spurious spatial spillover** from a mis-specified adjacency matrix, which Moran's I on residuals can help diagnose.

### Area 2 — Physics-Informed / mechanistic-model-informed neural networks for epidemiology

**Foundational:**
- **Raissi, Perdikaris & Karniadakis (2019), PINNs**, *Journal of Computational Physics* 378:686–707. The residual-loss framework `L = L_data + L_ODE`, where the physics loss penalizes solutions with large ODE/PDE residual.
- **Kermack & McKendrick (1927)** — original SIR compartmental model; the mechanistic ancestor of every term below.

**Epidemic-specific PINN / mechanistic-NN (strongest for justifying the physics-informed loss):**
- **Rodríguez, Cui, Ramakrishnan, Adhikari & Prakash (2023), EINNs**, "Epidemiologically-Informed Neural Networks," AAAI 2023, pp. 14453–14460 (arXiv:2202.10446). Leverages PINNs to learn latent epidemic dynamics and transfer them to a network that ingests heterogeneous data; notably "we do not assume the observability of complete dynamics and do not need to numerically solve the ODE equations during training," with clear benefits in both short- and long-term forecasting (reported improvements of up to 475% in correlation and up to 55% in accuracy over neural baselines for COVID-19). **Top reference for the physics-loss choice**, especially because dengue compartments (exposed humans, infected mosquitoes) are latent/unobserved.
- **Wang, Adiga, Chen, Sadilek, Venkatramanan & Marathe (2022), CausalGNN**, "Causal-Based Graph Neural Networks for Spatio-Temporal Epidemic Forecasting," AAAI 2022, 36(11):12191–12199, DOI 10.1609/aaai.v36i11.21479. A causal/mechanistic compartmental model guides GNN embedding learning via a mutual-learning mechanism in an attention-based dynamic GNN. **The closest GNN+physics prior art** and a template for coupling the two.
- **Liu, Wan, Prakash, Lau & Jin (2024), HeatGNN**, "Epidemiology-informed Graph Neural Network for Heterogeneity-aware Epidemic Forecasting," arXiv:2411.17372. Binds an SIR mechanistic model into a GNN to produce epidemiology-informed location embeddings and time-varying mechanistic-affinity graphs; a mechanistic simulation loss constrains representations toward epidemiological consistency.
- **Wan, Liu, Shan, Lau, Prakash & Jin (2024/ICML 2025), EARTH**, "Epidemiology-Aware Neural ODE with Continuous Disease Transmission Graph," arXiv:2410.00049 (PMLR v267, ICML 2025). SIR-inspired neural ODE (EANO) + Global-guided Local Transmission Graph for continuous, mechanistically-grounded regional spread.
- **Shaier, Raissi & Seshaiyer (2022), DINN**, "Disease-Informed Neural Networks," *Letters in Biomathematics* 9:71–105. Embeds SEIR-type ODEs into the NN loss (model loss + data loss).
- **Millevoi, Pasetto & Ferronato (2024)**, "A physics-informed neural network approach for compartmental epidemiological models," *PLOS Computational Biology* 20(9):e1012387.

**Reaction-diffusion PDE constraints (spatial spread):**
- **"Constraint-Aware Physics-Informed Neural Networks for SEIR Reaction-Diffusion Epidemic Models with Vital Dynamics"** (arXiv:2605.19886, preprint). The loss enforces non-negativity of compartment populations and admissibility of epidemiological parameters, with Fourier feature mapping to represent sharp epidemic fronts — a good template for **conservation-of-population and smoothness-across-neighboring-nodes soft constraints** on the district graph.

**Vector-borne (dengue) mechanistic models — the domain-specific physics for your loss:**
- **SEIR-SEI host-vector model**: SEIR for humans coupled to SEI for mosquitoes; "Analysis of Vector-host SEIR-SEI Dengue Epidemiological Model," *International Journal of Analysis and Applications* (2022), deriving R₀ via the next-generation method with stability analysis. Grounded in the classic **Ross-Macdonald** vector-borne framework. This is the natural mechanistic prior to embed as your residual loss.
- **Dengue-Informed Neural Network (2026)**, "Inferring unobserved vector dynamics for dengue forecasting using physics-informed neural networks and mechanistic transmission models," bioRxiv 2026.02.13.705693 (preprint). Integrates a reduced SI-SIR transmission model with monthly dengue incidence across 15 countries (2014–2025) via a PINN (DINN) to reconstruct the unobserved infected-mosquito time series, then forecasts with RNN/GRU/LSTM. **Directly relevant vector-borne PINN precedent** for handling latent mosquito compartments.
- **"A Fractional Physics-Informed Neural Network with Ensemble Learning for Dengue Forecasting"** (Springer, 2025, DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-29912-3_30). Fractional-order memory + PINN with ensemble averaging for dengue; reports improved stability, accuracy, and uncertainty estimation.
- **SEIR-SEI-EnKF (2021)**, IEEE (Xplore doc 9624940). Dengue SEIR-SEI compartments assimilated via ensemble Kalman filtering — a non-NN mechanistic forecasting comparator.
- **"An explainable covariate compartmental model for predicting the spatio-temporal patterns of dengue in Sri Lanka"** (PMC12500091). A covariate-driven SEIR + deep-learning hybrid on Sri Lanka data; strongest drivers were precipitation, socio-demographics, and NDVI — useful for feature selection and as a Sri Lanka-specific mechanistic comparator.

**Physics-informed GNN (PIGNN) — bridges Areas 1 & 2:**
- **Zhao/Gao et al. (2024/2025), PIGNN**, "Combining physics-informed graph neural network and finite difference for solving forward and inverse spatiotemporal PDEs," arXiv:2405.20000, *Computer Physics Communications* 309:109462. Integrates GNN + physical-law residual loss + finite-difference on irregular meshes; the headline result — "small area training, large area application" with strong time-extrapolation — directly supports formulating physics residuals over an irregular district graph.
- **Jiang et al., PhyGNNet**; **RBF-MGN** (arXiv:2212.02861) — additional spatiotemporal-PDE PINNs built on graph/mesh structures.

**Evidence physics-informed losses help under data scarcity / extrapolation:** EINN (long-horizon gains, latent-dynamics learning), PIGNN ("small area training, large area application"), and compartmental-model-guided COVID augmentation (arXiv:2302.01155) all report improved long-horizon/extrapolation performance and robustness under scarce, noisy data — the central argument for adding a mechanistic loss term to a data-scarce 25-node dengue problem.

### Area 3 — GAN-based / generative data augmentation for scarce time-series and graph data

**Foundational GAN / time-series GANs (strongest for justifying GAN augmentation):**
- **Goodfellow et al. (2014), GANs**, "Generative Adversarial Nets," NeurIPS 2014, arXiv:1406.2661 (DOI 10.5555/2969033.2969125). The foundational adversarial generator/discriminator framework, widely used to expand training sets and address small-sample problems.
- **Yoon, Jarrett & van der Schaar (2019), TimeGAN**, "Time-series Generative Adversarial Networks," NeurIPS 2019, vol. 32, pp. 5508–5518. Introduces, "in addition to the unsupervised adversarial loss on both real and synthetic sequences, a stepwise supervised loss," combined with a learned embedding space to preserve temporal dynamics — the standard benchmark for realistic multivariate time-series generation. **Top reference for the augmentation choice.**
- **Esteban, Hyland & Rätsch (2017), RCGAN**, "Real-valued (Medical) Time Series Generation with Recurrent Conditional GANs," arXiv:1706.02633. Conditional generation of realistic medical time series — the most directly health-relevant conditional-GAN precedent (condition generation on covariates/outbreak labels).
- **Lin, Jain, Wang, Fanti & Sekar (2020), DoppelGANger**, "Using GANs for Sharing Networked Time Series Data," ACM IMC 2020, DOI 10.1145/3419394.3423643 (arXiv:1909.13403). Handles multivariate signals + categorical metadata + long sequences; strong for structured augmentation.
- **SeriesGAN (2024)**, arXiv:2410.21203 — dual-discriminator + autoencoder-based loss + teacher-forcing supervisor to reduce mode collapse and embedding information loss; relevant mitigation design.

**GANs for epidemic / health time series (downstream-accuracy evidence):**
- **"Time-Series Prediction for the Epidemic Trends of COVID-19 Using Conditional GAN Regression"** (2022), *SN Computer Science*, DOI 10.1007/s42979-022-01225-7. CGAN regression forecasts country-wise COVID-19 cases three months ahead.
- **"Deep COVID-19 Forecasting for Multiple States with Data Augmentation"** (arXiv:2302.01155). Argues that generic GAN/data-augmentation "cannot reflect the epidemic nature" of the data and instead uses compartmental-model-guided augmentation — a **direct motivation for combining mechanistic priors with augmentation**, i.e., a mini-version of your project's thesis.
- **Wang et al. (2020), TDEFSI**, arXiv:2002.04663 — theory-guided deep learning that generates large volumes of synthetic epidemic training data to overcome sparse observations and overfitting.
- **Dengue-specific augmentation:** hybrid dengue models incorporating **TimeGAN** augmentation (Infectious Disease Modelling, ScienceDirect S2468042725001393, 2025) and ConvLSTM/ConvBiLSTM dengue forecasting with augmentation (*Computers* MDPI 2025, DOI 10.3390/computers15020114). Pure GAN augmentation dedicated to dengue is sparse — itself a supporting sub-gap for your review.

**Alternatives / complements (brief comparison):** diffusion models for time series (DAD4TS, arXiv:2605.17866 — superior sample quality/stability but high compute from many iterative steps); VAEs (TimeVAE — structured latent representation); and simple augmentation (jittering, window warping, slicing — Wen et al. 2020 survey, arXiv:2002.12478). GAN is preferred for its balance of fidelity, moderate compute (vs. diffusion), and controllability via conditional generation on covariates/outbreak labels — but a jittering/window-warping baseline should always be included to prove the GAN earns its complexity.

**Pitfalls on small data:** mode collapse and training instability are acute with ~520 weeks × 25 nodes. Documented mitigations: Wasserstein GAN + gradient penalty (WGAN-GP); conditional, gradient-penalized TimeGAN variants (e.g., CTGAN-MSIN, PMC12656402, which explicitly addresses TimeGAN's unstable training under imbalance); dual-discriminator designs (SeriesGAN); and — crucially — validating that augmentation *measurably improves downstream forecast error*, not just distributional similarity to real data.

### Area 4 — Combined / hybrid approaches (closest prior art; the novelty gap)

- **GNN + physics loss:** CausalGNN (AAAI 2022), HeatGNN (2024), EARTH (2024/ICML 2025), **TG-PhyNN** (arXiv:2408.16379; Springer LNCS DOI 10.1007/978-981-96-0119-6_4) — embeds differential-equation constraints as a training loss in a temporal GNN, tested on traffic, COVID-19, and chickenpox — and PIGNN (arXiv:2405.20000). This pairing is the most mature.
- **Physics-informed GANs:** **Daw, Maruf & Karpatne (2021), PID-GAN**, "A GAN Framework based on a Physics-informed Discriminator for Uncertainty Quantification with Physics," KDD 2021, DOI 10.1145/3447548.3467449 (arXiv:2106.02993). Physics informs both the generator and discriminator and it avoids the generator-gradient imbalance of multi-term losses. **Top reference for PI-GAN.** Also TrafficFlowGAN (physics-informed flow-based GAN for uncertainty quantification).
- **GNN + GAN (no physics):** Di-GraphGAN (traffic imputation + forecasting under missing data, *Information Sciences* 2024, DOI S0020025524008259); STG-GAN (rail passenger flow); GCN-GAN (traffic flow). Demonstrates the mechanics of coupling adversarial training with a graph backbone.

**Research gap (explicit):** To the best of current knowledge, **no published work integrates all three of {spatio-temporal GNN, epidemic-mechanistic (SEIR-SEI / host-vector) physics-informed loss, GAN-based augmentation} — and none does so for dengue.** The field has matured the pairwise combinations (GNN+physics: CausalGNN / HeatGNN / TG-PhyNN; GNN+GAN: Di-GraphGAN; physics+GAN: PID-GAN) but not the triple. Applying it to the Sri Lanka 25-district dataset — precisely where Weng et al. (2024) explicitly deferred methodological novelty — is a clean, conference-scale contribution. Because this is absence-of-evidence rather than proof, phrase the claim as "to the best of our knowledge."

### Area 5 — Evaluation practices and baselines

**Metrics.** Point accuracy: RMSE, MAE, MAPE (used across essentially all the dengue GNN papers above). Probabilistic: CRPS — which "represents a generalization of the absolute error to probabilistic forecasts" and reduces to the ranked probability score for integer counts (Bracher et al., "Evaluating epidemic forecasts in an interval format," *PLOS Computational Biology*, DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008618) — plus PICP (prediction-interval coverage probability) and MPIW (mean prediction-interval width) for calibration. Spatial: Moran's I on residuals to confirm the graph captures genuine spatial autocorrelation without spurious spillover.

**Temporal validation.** Use rolling-origin / expanding-window evaluation ("evaluation on a rolling forecasting origin," Hyndman & Athanasopoulos, *Forecasting: Principles and Practice* 3rd ed., §5.10): retrain at each origin using only data available up to that point, and average errors across origins; never use random shuffled splits (temporal leakage). SpatialEpiBench (arXiv:2605.06530) exemplifies a standardized rolling-origin retraining protocol for spatiotemporal epidemic forecasting and is a useful methodological citation.

**Baselines to include.** Naive/persistence and seasonal-naive; ARIMA/ARIMAX/SARIMA (Sri Lanka precedent: modified ARIMA for Colombo weekly dengue, PLOS One DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0299953; and the Gampaha time-series regression model, DOI 10.1186/s13071-018-2828-2); Random Forest and XGBoost; LSTM/GRU/ConvLSTM; and GNN baselines GCN, GAT, Graph WaveNet, DCRNN, STGCN, EpiGNN/ColaGNN. The ablation should isolate: (a) GNN alone, (b) GNN + physics loss, (c) GNN + GAN augmentation, (d) the full triple — this ablation table is what substantiates the novelty claim.

## Recommendations

1. **Anchor on the exact baseline.** Cite Weng et al. (2024) as the explicit baseline and gap statement; reproduce their GCN/GAT + distance-based adjacency on the same Sri Lanka dataset first, then frame novelty as architecture + loss + augmentation on that identical benchmark. Upgrading their fixed distance adjacency to a learned/adaptive adjacency (Graph WaveNet) is a low-risk first novelty increment.
2. **Physics-informed loss (Stage 1 novelty).** Implement an SEIR-SEI host-vector residual (grounded in Ross-Macdonald; cite EINN, CausalGNN, the dengue DINN, and the SEIR-SEI model) as a soft constraint: `L = L_data + λ_phys·L_residual + λ_cons·L_population-conservation + λ_smooth·L_neighbor-smoothness`. Report λ-sensitivity. **Benchmark threshold:** the physics loss should improve 3-step-ahead RMSE and out-of-distribution (peak-season) accuracy vs. the GNN-only model; if it does not, lower λ_phys or move the mechanism to latent-dynamics transfer (EINN-style) rather than a hard residual, since dengue's exposed-human and infected-mosquito compartments are unobserved.
3. **GAN augmentation (Stage 2 novelty).** Start with TimeGAN or a conditional GAN (RCGAN-style, conditioned on meteorological covariates and outbreak labels); always include a jittering/window-warping baseline and, if time permits, a VAE or diffusion comparator. **Decision threshold:** retain augmentation only if it measurably improves downstream RMSE/CRPS on held-out rolling origins — not merely distributional similarity. Use WGAN-GP / gradient penalty and monitor explicitly for mode collapse on the ~520×25 dataset.
4. **Stretch goal — tightest integration.** Adopt a PID-GAN-style coupling in which the epidemic-physics residual informs the GAN discriminator, so generated series are epidemiologically consistent. This most directly fuses all three pillars and maximizes the novelty claim.
5. **Evaluation.** Rolling-origin CV; report RMSE/MAE/MAPE + CRPS + PICP/MPIW + Moran's I; present the full four-way ablation. Include ARIMA/SARIMA, XGBoost, LSTM, and GCN/GAT/Graph WaveNet baselines.
6. **Extension for generalization.** Validate transferability on OpenDengue (Clarke, Lim, Gupte, Pigott, van Panhuis & Brady, *Scientific Data* 2024, 11(1):296, DOI 10.1038/s41597-024-03120-7) — over 56 million dengue cases from 102 countries (1924–2023), with >95% of records at weekly/monthly resolution and subnational data for 40 countries, drawn from 843 sources — to show the method generalizes beyond Sri Lanka.

## Caveats
- **Preprint / very-recent status.** Several of the most on-point references are not yet peer-reviewed or carry future-dated identifiers: the Siabi et al. *Scientific Reports* dengue STGCN and DengueGNN carry 2026 DOIs; the dengue DINN (bioRxiv 2026.02.13.705693), the Constraint-Aware SEIR reaction-diffusion PINN (arXiv:2605.19886), and some augmentation/benchmark preprints (arXiv 2605.x/2606.x) are unrefereed. Verify each ID, venue, and metadata directly on the publisher/arXiv before citing, and treat preprint performance claims as provisional.
- **Novelty claim is absence-of-evidence.** The "no triple combination exists" finding comes from targeted search, not exhaustive proof — phrase as "to the best of our knowledge."
- **Weng et al. citation.** It surfaces as a NASA Technical Reports PDF tied to IEEE BigData 2024; confirm the exact author list, title, and venue/pages before submission.
- **GAN risk on small data.** GAN augmentation on ~520 weekly timesteps is genuinely prone to mode collapse and instability; keep a simple augmentation fallback (jittering/window-warping) ready so the contribution does not hinge solely on GAN success.
- **LLM/foundation-model exclusion respected.** All recommended components (GNN, mechanistic ODE loss, GAN) satisfy the course constraint that novelty come from architecture, loss, data representation, or training strategy rather than large foundation models.
