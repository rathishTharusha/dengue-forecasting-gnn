\begin{abstract}
Spatio-temporal graph neural networks are the default choice for district-level
epidemic forecasting, but on small graphs their advantage is rarely tested against
naive baselines. We benchmark them on Sri Lankan dengue --- 25 districts, 459
weeks --- under leakage-free rolling-origin cross-validation with eight disjoint
origins, and report three findings. First, no graph configuration we tested beats
a persistence forecast; the best is statistically indistinguishable from it and
the rest are significantly worse. Second, a learned adaptive adjacency appears to
help at 24 paired runs and does not replicate at 64, while a gated temporal
convolution improves RMSE from 61.8 to 56.8 and a constraint derived from the
generation interval improves it further, both at $p<0.001$. Third, a conditional
GAN for data augmentation is significantly worse than jittering. Peak-timing error
exposes a gap that squared error conceals.
\end{abstract}
