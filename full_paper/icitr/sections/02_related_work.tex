\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{ST-GNNs for epidemics.} Spatio-temporal graph networks couple a graph
operator over regions with a temporal operator over history. STGAT and
A3TGCN~\cite{bai2021a3tgcn} use attention, ASTGCN~\cite{guo2019astgcn} attends over
space and time, AAGCN~\cite{shi2019aagcn} adapts its adjacency,
DCRNN~\cite{li2018dcrnn} diffuses over the graph inside a recurrent cell, and Graph
WaveNet~\cite{wu2019graphwavenet} learns the adjacency from node embeddings.
Cola-GNN~\cite{deng2020colagnn} and EpiGNN~\cite{xie2022epignn} adapt such models to
epidemic forecasting with cross-location attention and transmission-risk encodings. Weng et al.~\cite{weng2024graph} benchmark five traffic ST-GNNs
on Sri Lankan dengue, and GulMohamed et al.~\cite{gulmohamed2026denguegnn} add
per-horizon heads and temporal attention. These models predict the case level
directly, and the dengue benchmark reports no persistence baseline.

\textbf{Mechanistic hybrids.} SEIR-LSTM~\cite{liu2025seirlstm} predicts the force of
infection of an SEIR model with an LSTM. EINNs~\cite{rodriguez2023einns} share latent
dynamics between a neural and a mechanistic model, while
CausalGNN~\cite{wang2022causalgnn} and MepoGNN~\cite{cao2023mepognn} embed a
metapopulation SEIR in a graph network. Physics-informed
networks~\cite{raissi2019pinn,nguyen2024mppinn} add the dynamics as a loss. These works
compare against purely data-driven models, but not against the same head with the
simulator removed, so the share of the gain due to physics is not measured.

\textbf{Simple baselines.} In classical forecasting the naive forecast is the reference
every method must beat, and modelling the change rather than the level is the standard
treatment of a near-random-walk series~\cite{hyndman2021forecasting}. In deep
spatio-temporal forecasting, STID~\cite{shao2022stid} shows that identity embeddings
can match graph operators. \model{} brings the classical idea into the ST-GNN head.
