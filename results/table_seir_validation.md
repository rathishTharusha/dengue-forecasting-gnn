## 3. The growth ceiling

`ln(R0)/GI` assumes every onward infection lands exactly one generation interval later. The model's stage durations are exponential, and for a given R0 that produces faster early growth than a fixed delay -- so the naive formula understates the bound.

| R0 cap | max lambda /day | max weekly log growth | naive ln(R0)/3wk | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 0.10186 | 0.7130 | 0.2310 | 3.09x |
| 3 | 0.17328 | 1.2130 | 0.3662 | 3.31x |
| 4 | 0.23572 | 1.6500 | 0.4621 | 3.57x |
| 6 | 0.34121 | 2.3884 | 0.5973 | 4.00x |
| 8 | 0.42031 | 2.9422 | 0.6931 | 4.24x |
| 10 | 0.45201 | 3.1641 | 0.7675 | 4.12x |

At the chosen cap R0 <= 6 the ceiling is **2.3884** per week, attained at b=1, beta_h=0.75, beta_v=0.75, m=10, 1/mu_v=7.0d, 1/nu_v=10.0d, 1/nu_h=4.0d, 1/gamma_h=2.0d (R0 = 5.69).

### Against the observed data

11450 district-week growth rates, 25 districts:

| threshold | observations above | share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| old guessed ceiling 0.70 | 2394 | 20.91% |
| derived 2.3884 | 122 | 1.07% |

The old ceiling declared a fifth of the recorded data physically impossible. A constraint that a fifth of the observations violate is not encoding physics; it is penalising forecasts for tracking real outbreak dynamics, which is the behaviour we most want. That is the likeliest explanation for the band term never helping in Stage 2b or Stage 3.
