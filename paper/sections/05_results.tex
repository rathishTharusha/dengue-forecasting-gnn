\section{Architectures and Increments}
\label{sec:results}

\cA{We evaluate the five reproduced architectures under the protocol of
\S\ref{sec:setup}, each with four increments justified by prior work or by our own
exploratory analysis: per-horizon output heads, additive temporal attention and a
Gaussian predictive head~\cite{gulmohamed2026denguegnn}, and a Huber objective
motivated by the target's skewness. All five architectures accept all four
increments through a shared output seam, so no cell of the table covers an
unstated subset.}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_results}
  \caption{Every arm against the persistence floor, scored both ways. AAGCN's
  3.8-point lead in (a) does not survive in (b): it was a lead at predicting a
  reporting backlog. Bars are means over 3 origins $\times$ 3 seeds.}
  \label{fig:results}
\end{figure*}

\cA{Figure~\ref{fig:results} gives the result. Measured
over all windows, AAGCN (40.99) appears to beat the 44.80 floor by 3.8 points and
ASTGCN by 1.4. Measured artifact-free, AAGCN sits at 29.84 against a floor of
29.52---\emph{above} it---and the ordering of architectures changes. The apparent
winner was the architecture that best absorbed six windows of backlogged
reporting.}

\cB{Artifact-free, only STGAT (29.35) and A3TGCN (29.05) are below the floor at
all, by 0.6\% and 1.6\%. Under paired $t$-tests on matched (architecture, origin,
seed) runs, \textbf{no increment differs from its own baseline at $p<0.05$}:
per-horizon heads $+0.526$ ($p=0.078$), temporal attention $+0.245$ ($p=0.077$),
Huber $+0.128$ ($p=0.462$), Gaussian head $+0.280$ ($p=0.304$). The two smallest
$p$-values belong to increments that are \emph{worse} than baseline. We report
this as the null it is; selecting the minimum of a table this noisy would
manufacture a result.}

\cB{One increment does earn its place, on a different axis. The Gaussian head
yields 95\% prediction intervals with empirical coverage between 0.947 and 0.962
across all five architectures---calibrated intervals that a point forecast cannot
supply, and the operationally useful output of the whole study.}

\cC{We also tested a learned adjacency in place of the geographic one. Enabling
AAGCN's adaptive branch costs $+2.76$ RMSE artifact-free on matched folds and
seeds (7/9 worse). A controlled comparison over identity, fixed, learned and
blended adjacencies is confounded until an explicit self-connection is restored,
after which a blended graph edges ahead of both extremes ($28.55$ against a
fixed-graph $28.97$); at $n=9$ we report that as unsettled.}

\section{The Mechanistic Prior}
\label{sec:physics}

\cD{The project's intended contribution was a physics-informed loss from the
SEIR--SEI model. A hard compartmental residual is not identifiable here---seven
compartments with exactly one observed, and that one under-ascertained---so we use
the mechanistic identity that acts on the observable, the renewal equation
\begin{equation}
  y_t \;=\; R_t \sum_{s\ge 1} w_s \, y_{t-s},
  \label{eq:renewal}
\end{equation}
where the generation-interval distribution $w$ follows from the SEIR--SEI stage
durations of Phaijoo and Gurung~\cite{phaijoo2018sensitivity}, whose nine
sensitivity indices we reproduce. The implied mean generation interval is 3.30
weeks. Only the stage durations are used; their $R_0$ is not, since generation
interval is disease biology and transmission intensity is local.}

\cD{Equation~\eqref{eq:renewal} describes the record well. Given oracle $R_t$, a
three-week replay scores RMSE \textbf{13.44} where persistence scores 55.03 on the
same windows. We tested it both as a soft penalty and as a decoder in which the
network emits $\log R_t$ and cases are reconstructed through the mechanism. The
decoder does exactly what it was built to do---maximum predicted weekly log-growth
rises from 0.07 to 4.14, so the flatness is gone---and is 6 RMSE worse anyway. The
penalty degrades RMSE monotonically in its weight.}

\cE{Table~\ref{tab:physics} localises the failure. Every causally available
anchor loses to persistence: force alone ($R{=}1$) scores
35.45, a damped ratio form 33.05, and $\Rhat\cdot\text{force}$ 50.01, against
29.52. The predictive content of Eq.~\eqref{eq:renewal} is entirely in $R_t$---and
$R_t$ is only 26\% predictable out of sample, from its own past and from nothing
else. With $\mathrm{sd}(\log R)=0.749$, that leaves a residual multiplicative
error of $\exp(0.64)=\mathbf{1.90\times}$ per step, compounding over three weeks.
Persistence carries no such factor. \emph{Reconstructing a forecast through $R$
re-injects a factor-of-two error where the naive baseline had none.}}

\cE{We tested the two mechanisms that could have supplied the missing
predictability. \textbf{Susceptible depletion}---the mechanism by which an SEIR
epidemic turns over---adds exactly nothing beyond $R$'s own past ($r^2$
$0.263\rightarrow0.263$). Its magnitude explains why: even at 1-in-20
under-ascertainment, cumulative infection over the whole 8.8-year record moves
$\log R$ by 0.618, i.e.\ $\approx0.0013$ per week, against a weekly standard
deviation of 0.749. Sri Lankan dengue is endemic in a population of 21.9M that
does not run out of susceptibles on a three-week horizon. \textbf{Climate driving
$R_0$} fails too: the sensitivity indices ($b$ $+1.000$, $\mu_v$ $-0.818$, $m$
$+0.500$, $\nu_v$ $+0.318$) imply a hump-shaped thermal response that a linear fit
must miss, and allowing that curvature changes out-of-sample $r^2$ from $-0.025$
to $-0.028$. We did not fit entomological response curves to our own data, since a
mechanistic constant estimated from the series it then constrains is not a prior.}

\cF{Table~\ref{tab:physics} also gives the sign error directly: $\log\Rhat_t$
correlates $+0.798$ with the \emph{past} three weeks of growth and $-0.217$ with
the next three. It is a backward-looking state estimate, so a penalty pulling a
forecast toward it pulls the wrong way---which is the monotonic damage we measured.
A physics-informed \emph{variance} term fails for the same reason and with the
same sign: $\log\Rhat_t$ correlates $-0.106$ with the magnitude of future growth,
so elevated $\Rhat$ does not even signal elevated uncertainty.}

\subsection{The ceiling is informational}
\cF{Regressing each model's predicted log-growth on realised log-growth gives a
slope of \textbf{0.002}. The models' growth predictions carry no information about
growth; they are persistence with noise. Under squared error this is
\emph{correct}: the target is a residual over a baseline that already explains
$r^2=0.85$, and the conditional mean of what remains is approximately zero.
Flatness is what the objective asks for. This is why an asymmetric loss, the
renewal penalty and the renewal decoder each bought responsiveness and paid for it
in RMSE, in the same direction. An asymmetric loss weighted $3\times$ against
under-prediction removes the outbreak bias entirely ($-10.58 \rightarrow +1.21$
cases) and costs 2.8 RMSE, because the model cannot tell which windows are
outbreaks and so inflates everywhere.}

\section{The Target That Is Tractable}
\label{sec:detection}

\cA{The failure above is specific to \emph{counting}. Ranking is a different
question, and the operationally relevant one: which districts will exceed their
alert threshold. We define an outbreak as a district-week above that district's
training-set 90th percentile anywhere in the horizon (base rate 14.4\%) and fit a
logistic model on training weeks only.}

\cA{Current level alone reaches AUC \textbf{0.807} (average precision 0.598
against a 14.4\% base rate). Adding $\Rhat$ raises it to
\textbf{0.826}---a larger gain than recent growth ($+0.004$) or neighbouring
districts ($+0.006$), and climate again subtracts. Before onset, mean $\Rhat$ runs
1.13--1.24 for six weeks against a global median of 0.911: sustained $R>1$ is the
textbook signature of a growing epidemic, and it is visible before the outbreak is.
This is where the mechanistic prior belongs.}
