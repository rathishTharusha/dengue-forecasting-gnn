\begin{abstract}
Spatio-temporal graph neural networks (ST-GNNs) and SEIR-informed neural models
are both proposed for district-level dengue forecasting, but they are rarely
compared under one leak-free protocol. We study whether replacing the LSTM of an
SEIR-informed forecaster with a graph encoder helps, on Sri Lanka's weekly record
(25 districts, three-week horizon). Before modelling, we audit the benchmark
array used by prior work. Its rows are out of date order, every training split
contains 2023 data from after the test periods, its case and climate columns drift
apart by up to a year, its ``lagged'' climate channels hold values 12--17 weeks in
the \emph{future}, and its largest spike is a spreadsheet error. We rebuild a
559-week dataset from the source reports and enforce causal lags with tests.
On one harness, six encoders (five published ST-GNNs and the SEIR-LSTM's LSTM)
are each paired with a direct head, a pure SEIR decoder and a gated SEIR head.
The architecture matters little: working encoders span 0.15 RMSE, and the graph
adds 0.07. The pure SEIR decoder loses 6--7 RMSE on every encoder. The cause is
structural: 14--16\% of targets lie below its zero-transmission floor, and its
force of infection is only $r^2\approx0.25$ predictable. The gated SEIR head behaves
differently. It brings all six encoders within 0.25 of each other and below
persistence, and repairs the three that fail as published by 5.7--17.2
validation RMSE on 9/9 paired runs. A negative-binomial likelihood is the
largest controlled gain ($-0.41$, 9/9). The graph beats the LSTM only inside the
gated SEIR head (9/9 on three origins), and that advantage does not replicate on
nine disjoint origins. More than twenty further levers are not adopted:
architecture, embeddings, climate lags, synthetic data, a spatial physics penalty
and a metapopulation SEIR head. Their residuals correlate 0.87--0.99. On this
target, physics helps a graph network as a stabilising structural prior, not as
a forecaster.
\end{abstract}
