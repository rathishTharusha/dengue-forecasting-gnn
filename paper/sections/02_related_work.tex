\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

% --- Thread 1: dengue forecasting on this dataset -- 65 words --------------
\noindent\textbf{Spatio-temporal GNNs for dengue.}
Weng et al.~\cite{weng2024graph} assembled the Sri Lanka district dataset and
showed that spatio-temporal graph models --- among them DCRNN~\cite{li2018dcrnn}
--- outperform ARIMA, Random Forest, XGBoost and LSTM on it. Their evaluation
uses a single chronological split and reports no naive baseline, and they
identify neighbour-driven spurious spikes as an unresolved limitation. We adopt
their dataset and address both gaps.

% --- Thread 2: adaptive / dynamic graph structure -- 65 words --------------
\noindent\textbf{Learned graph structure.}
Graph WaveNet~\cite{wu2019graphwavenet} learns a self-adaptive adjacency from
node embeddings for traffic forecasting, and EpiGNN~\cite{xie2022epignn} and
Cola-GNN~\cite{deng2020colagnn} learn location attention for influenza. Closest
to our setting, DengueGNN~\cite{gulmohamed2026denguegnn} builds a dynamic
adjacency by blending geographic proximity with a gravity-model mobility prior.
Sri Lanka has no reliable inter-district mobility data, so we infer connectivity
from the series themselves rather than assuming its generating process.

\noindent\textbf{Constrained objectives.}
Physics-informed neural networks~\cite{raissi2019pinn} embed governing equations
in the training loss; EINNs~\cite{rodriguez2023einns} and
CausalGNN~\cite{wang2022causalgnn} carry this idea into epidemic forecasting for
influenza and COVID-19, where compartment trajectories are comparatively well
observed. Dengue's exposed-human and infected-mosquito compartments are
not~\cite{seirsei2022}, and no prior work constrains a district-level dengue GNN
toward SEIR--SEI dynamics. We do not close that gap here. We test the weaker
constraint first --- spatial smoothness over the graph, which requires no
unobserved compartments --- and report that it does not help, which bears on
whether the stronger one is worth the modelling cost.
