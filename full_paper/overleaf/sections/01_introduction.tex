\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\cA{Dengue is the fastest-spreading mosquito-borne viral disease globally, and Sri
Lanka faces recurring severe epidemics: the 2017 outbreak alone recorded over
186{,}000 cases and 440 deaths~\cite{tissera2020severe}. Roughly three weeks
separate infection from clinical diagnosis, so district-level forecasts issued
$H=3$ weeks ahead carry actionable lead time for pre-positioning clinical capacity
and intensifying vector control.}

\cB{Three properties shape the task. Transmission is spatially coupled, which
motivates a graph; the panel is small (25 districts, 459 weeks) so parameters are
cheap to overfit; and the target is severely heavy-tailed (skewness 8.6, the top
1\% of district-weeks holding 18.3\% of cases), which makes squared error a
measurement of a few dozen windows.}

\cC{A fourth property is easy to miss and dominates the rest: weekly incidence is
strongly autocorrelated. Cases at $t-1$ explain $r^2=0.85$ of cases at $t$. Any
model is therefore competing against a naive persistence forecast
$\hat{y}_{t+h}=y_t$ that is already close to optimal, and a benchmark that omits
that baseline cannot tell whether its architecture contributed anything. Weng
et al.~\cite{weng2024graph}, the reference benchmark for this dataset, omit it.}

\cD{We proposed three stackable contributions to an ST-GNN baseline: (a) a
physics-informed loss from an SEIR--SEI compartmental model, as
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}} + \lambda_p\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}} +
\lambda_c\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cons}} + \lambda_s\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{smooth}}$;
(b) GAN augmentation; and (c) a learned adaptive graph. This paper reports (a) and
(c); (b) is left to the full study. Establishing what the baseline actually
\emph{was} turned out to be the harder problem, and it reframes the target. Cases
at $t-1$ explain $r^2=0.85$ of cases at $t$, so \emph{how many} is close to
answered before a model starts; \emph{when}---which district crosses an alert
threshold, and roughly when---is not. That is the question early warning actually
asks, and it is where we find the mechanism earns its place.}

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
  \item \textbf{Exact reproduction.} Five published ST-GNNs to within 7.3\%
        across twenty comparisons; the reported column is training-inclusive and
        per-window averaged, and naive persistence beats every model in it
        (\S\ref{sec:repro}).
  \item \textbf{An artifact-aware protocol.} A 19$\times$ reporting backlog at
        week~395 occupies one fold's test split, carries $\sim$90\% of its squared
        error and inverts fold difficulty; we score every arm and the baseline
        with and without it (\S\ref{sec:setup}).
  \item \textbf{A null result, stated as one.} Over 3 origins $\times$ 3 seeds, no
        architecture and no increment differs from its own baseline at $p<0.05$
        against an artifact-free floor of 29.52 (\S\ref{sec:results}).
  \item \textbf{Why the mechanistic prior cannot help, and where it can.}
        $R_t$ is 26\% predictable, implying a 1.90$\times$ error per step
        (\S\ref{sec:physics}); the same quantity is the largest single contributor
        to ranking outbreaks three weeks ahead (\S\ref{sec:constraint}).
\end{itemize}
