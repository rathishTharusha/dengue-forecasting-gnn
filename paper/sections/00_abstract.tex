\begin{abstract}
Dengue forecasting at district resolution is spatially coupled, data-scarce and
heavy-tailed, and spatio-temporal graph neural networks applied to it are known to
propagate outbreaks into districts where none occurred. Their adjacency is
typically fixed by geography, a proxy no mobility data supports. We present
\modelname, which learns district connectivity end-to-end and blends it with the
geographic graph under a learned gate, and we evaluate it against a matched
fixed-graph control under leakage-free rolling-origin cross-validation. The
learned graph improves RMSE from 45.47 to 45.00, but neither model clears a naive
persistence forecast at 44.80, which wins four of nine paired runs against the
best configuration. Adding spatial smoothness regularisation degrades accuracy at
every weight tested. Measuring outbreak peak displacement rather than error
magnitude exposes the more consequential gap: both graph models call the peak two
to three weeks later than persistence does.
\end{abstract}
