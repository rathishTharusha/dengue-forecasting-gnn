\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% --- (a)+(b) Problem and why it matters -- 75 words ------------------------
Dengue is the fastest-spreading mosquito-borne viral disease in the world, and Sri
Lanka carries a heavy and growing burden: the 2017 epidemic alone produced more
than 186{,}000 reported cases and 440 deaths~\cite{tissera2020severe}. Because
roughly three weeks separate infection from diagnosis, district-level forecasts
issued several weeks ahead are directly actionable. They give the National Dengue
Control Unit and regional health teams time to pre-position resources, intensify
larval-source reduction, and issue warnings before an outbreak peaks.

% --- (c) Why it is hard -- 80 words ---------------------------------------
Three difficulties make this forecasting task hard. Transmission is spatially
coupled: incidence in one district depends both on its own climatic and
epidemiological history and on infection spilling in from neighbours through human
movement, which non-spatial models cannot represent. The data are scarce --- 25
districts of weekly counts over roughly a decade, against the 207 nodes of a
standard traffic benchmark --- so deep models overfit readily. Finally, the target
is heavy-tailed, with a median of 13 weekly cases against a maximum of 2{,}631.

% --- (d) What is wrong with prior work -- 85 words. THE KEY PARAGRAPH. -----
% This paragraph is the paper's motivation and it comes from the predecessor's own
% admission. See docs/PHASE2_PLAN.md §1.1. Paraphrased, not quoted -- the lecture
% slides note that scientific writing rarely uses direct quotation.
Graph neural networks address the first difficulty, and Weng et
al.~\cite{weng2024graph} show that they outperform traditional early-warning
systems on this exact dataset. The same work, however, reports that its forecasts
in one district are driven by neighbouring districts even where no transmission
occurred, producing spurious spikes that the authors attribute to an inherent
limitation of graph models. Nothing in a purely data-driven objective prevents
this: the network is never told that its forecasts must correspond to a
physically realisable epidemic.

% --- (e) Our approach in brief -- 62 words --------------------------------
We propose PIAG-Net, which addresses the problem from both ends. Connectivity is
learned from the series themselves rather than fixed by inter-district distance,
removing a proxy that no mobility data supports. Training then adds a soft
SEIR--SEI host--vector constraint that penalises forecasts no plausible epidemic
could produce. We evaluate under leakage-free rolling-origin
cross-validation~\cite{hyndman2021forecasting} against both our own GCN/GAT
baseline and naive persistence.

% --- (f) Contributions -- 70 words. One line each. -------------------------
\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We show that a tuned spatio-temporal GNN only \emph{matches} a naive
        persistence forecast on this dataset (RMSE 45.3 vs.\ 44.8) --- a
        comparison absent from prior work on it.
  \item We learn district connectivity end-to-end, without assuming a mobility law.
  \item We constrain a spatio-temporal GNN toward host--vector dynamics through a
        soft SEIR--SEI training objective.
  \item We isolate each component in an ablation under one frozen protocol.
\end{itemize}
