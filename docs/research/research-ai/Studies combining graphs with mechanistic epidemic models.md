## Studies combining graphs with mechanistic epidemic models

The literature contains several distinct integration patterns; they should not all be called “physics-informed GNNs.”

- **SIRGCN / domain-differential-equation-informed GCNs.** Sun et al. embed a disease-propagation differential equation into a graph convolutional network and propose SIRGCN. Their stated motivation is robustness under train–test distribution shifts, and they report better robustness than domain-agnostic deep-learning baselines [^1].
- **SEIR-DST for dengue.** Chou et al. combine SEIR-based epidemiological simulation with spatio-temporal deep learning, attention, and dilated temporal convolutions for district-level dengue prediction in Tainan. The retrieved record establishes mechanistic integration, but does not specify an explicit ODE-residual penalty; it may therefore be better described as an SEIR-informed graph forecaster than a strict PINN [^2].
- **Dengue STGCN using SEIR–SEI-generated dynamics.** A 2024 study reports that an STGCN captures synthetic patterns generated by an SEIR–SEI model and adds mobility and weather features. This is evidence of mechanistic data generation/validation, but the available record does not say that SEIR–SEI residuals were included in the STGCN objective [^3].
- **Higher-order network PINN for dengue.** Gao and Zhu construct reaction–diffusion and reaction–advection–diffusion systems on higher-order networks, apply PINNs to dengue data from 49 Pan-American countries, use geographic/air-route/maritime networks to define diffusion and advection operators, and forecast 52 weeks ahead [^4]. This is the closest retrieved example to a graph-coupled dengue PINN, although its equations are reaction–diffusion/advection systems rather than a standard SEIR–SEI compartment model.
- **Physics-informed GNNs with discrete differentiation.** PIGNN combines GNNs, physical equations, and finite differences for forward/inverse spatio-temporal PDEs, including irregular meshes and long time steps. It is not an epidemiology-specific paper, but it provides a directly relevant implementation pattern for graph-based residuals without relying entirely on automatic differentiation [^5].

## How the physics loss is integrated with GNN training

Let $$\mathcal G=(\mathcal V,\mathcal E,\mathbf A)$$ be a spatial graph, with one node per district, city, or country. Let $$\mathbf z_{i,t}$$ contain observed cases, climate, mobility, and demographic features at node $$i$$ and week $$t$$. A spatio-temporal GNN produces compartment states or future states:

$$
\hat{\mathbf X}_{t+1:t+H}
=F_\theta\left(\mathbf X_{t-L+1:t},\mathbf Z_{t-L+1:t},\mathbf A\right).
$$

The usual supervised forecasting loss is

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{data}}
=\frac{1}{|\mathcal V|H}\sum_{i,t,h}
\left\|\mathbf M\hat{\mathbf x}_{i,t+h}-\mathbf y_{i,t+h}\right\|^2,
$$

where $$\mathbf M$$ maps latent compartments to observed outputs, such as reported incidence.

The physics-informed objective adds a residual term:

$$
\mathcal L(\theta,\phi)
=\lambda_d\mathcal L_{\mathrm{data}}
+\lambda_p\mathcal L_{\mathrm{phys}}
+\lambda_0\mathcal L_{\mathrm{IC}}
+\lambda_c\mathcal L_{\mathrm{cons}}
+\lambda_+\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pos}}
+\lambda_r\mathcal L_{\mathrm{prior}}.
$$

Here $$\theta$$ are GNN weights and $$\phi$$ are epidemiological parameters, which may be fixed or learned.

There are four main ways to define $$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{phys}}$$:

### 1. Continuous automatic-differentiation residual

For a node-level ODE/PDE system,

$$
\partial_t\mathbf x_i(t)
=\mathbf f\big(\mathbf x_i(t),\mathbf x_{\mathcal N(i)}(t),\mathbf u_i(t);\phi\big),
$$

compute

$$
\mathbf r_{i}(t)
=\partial_t\hat{\mathbf x}_i(t)
-\mathbf f\big(\hat{\mathbf x}_i(t),\hat{\mathbf x}_{\mathcal N(i)}(t),\mathbf u_i(t);\phi\big),
$$

and penalize

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{phys}}
=\frac{1}{N_c|\mathcal V|}
\sum_{i,j}\left\|\mathbf r_i(t_j^c)\right\|^2.
$$

This requires the GNN to accept continuous time or to interpolate its latent states between weekly observations. It is closest to a classical PINN.

### 2. Discrete one-step consistency loss

For weekly data, a practical alternative is to use the compartment model’s one-week flow map $$\Phi_{\Delta t}$$:

$$
\mathbf r_{i,k}^{\mathrm{flow}}
=\hat{\mathbf x}_{i,k+1}
-\Phi_{\Delta t}\left(\hat{\mathbf x}_{i,k},\hat{\mathbf x}_{\mathcal N(i),k},\mathbf u_{i,k};\phi\right).
$$

Then

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{phys}}
=\frac{1}{(|\mathcal V|)(K-1)}
\sum_{i,k}\left\|\mathbf r_{i,k}^{\mathrm{flow}}\right\|^2.
$$

$$\Phi_{\Delta t}$$ can be a differentiable Euler, Heun, RK4, or implicit solver. This approach is often more natural than estimating derivatives from weekly observations because it enforces the integrated dynamics over the full week.

### 3. Finite-difference residual on the weekly graph sequence

If the GNN outputs only weekly states, a centered residual is

$$
\mathbf r_{i,k}^{\mathrm{FD}}
=\frac{\hat{\mathbf x}_{i,k+1}-\hat{\mathbf x}_{i,k-1}}{2\Delta t}
-\mathbf f\big(\hat{\mathbf x}_{i,k},\hat{\mathbf x}_{\mathcal N(i),k};\phi\big).
$$

This is simple and matches discrete graph forecasting. It is less robust at endpoints and can be inaccurate when the weekly step is large or the epidemic has sharp peaks. The PIGNN literature explicitly combines GNNs, physical equations, and finite differences to obtain a discrete physics-informed method [^5].

### 4. Graph-diffusion or mobility residual

For spatial spread, define a graph operator such as a normalized mobility Laplacian $$\mathbf L_A$$. A node-level compartment equation can include spatial movement:

$$
\frac{d\mathbf x_i}{dt}
=\mathbf f_{\mathrm{SEIR-SEI}}(\mathbf x_i;\phi_i)
+D\sum_j A_{ij}(\mathbf x_j-\mathbf x_i).
$$

The residual becomes

$$
\mathbf r_i
=\partial_t\hat{\mathbf x}_i
-\mathbf f_{\mathrm{SEIR-SEI}}(\hat{\mathbf x}_i)
-D(\mathbf L_A\hat{\mathbf X})_i.
$$

This separates two mechanisms: within-node host–mosquito transmission is represented by the compartment equations, whereas human movement or spatial coupling is represented by the graph operator. Gao and Zhu use network-derived higher-order diffusion and advection operators for a dengue PINN, illustrating this general design [^4].

## Incorporating SEIR–SEI into a spatio-temporal GNN

For each node $$i$$, define

$$
\mathbf x_i=
(S_{h,i},E_{h,i},I_{h,i},R_{h,i},S_{v,i},E_{v,i},I_{v,i})^\top.
$$

A dengue host–vector system with optional graph coupling is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot S_{h,i}&=\Lambda_{h,i}-\lambda_{h,i}S_{h,i}-\mu_hS_{h,i}+D_h(\mathbf L_A S_h)_i,\\
\dot E_{h,i}&=\lambda_{h,i}S_{h,i}-(\sigma_h+\mu_h)E_{h,i}+D_h(\mathbf L_A E_h)_i,\\
\dot I_{h,i}&=\sigma_hE_{h,i}-(\gamma_h+\mu_h)I_{h,i}+D_h(\mathbf L_A I_h)_i,\\
\dot R_{h,i}&=\gamma_hI_{h,i}-\mu_hR_{h,i}+D_h(\mathbf L_A R_h)_i,\\
\dot S_{v,i}&=\Lambda_{v,i}-\lambda_{v,i}S_{v,i}-\mu_{v,i}S_{v,i},\\
\dot E_{v,i}&=\lambda_{v,i}S_{v,i}-(\sigma_v+\mu_{v,i})E_{v,i},\\
\dot I_{v,i}&=\sigma_vE_{v,i}-\mu_{v,i}I_{v,i},
\end{aligned}
$$

with

$$
\lambda_{h,i}=a_i b_{vh,i}\frac{I_{v,i}}{N_{h,i}},
\qquad
\lambda_{v,i}=a_i b_{hv,i}\frac{I_{h,i}}{N_{h,i}}.
$$

The GNN can be designed in either of two ways:

1. **State-output design:** the GNN directly predicts all seven compartments. Reported cases are decoded from the incidence flow, for example $$\hat C_{i,k}=\rho_i\int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}}\lambda_{h,i}(t)S_{h,i}(t)dt$$.
2. **Residual-correction design:** a mechanistic SEIR–SEI simulator produces a baseline trajectory, and the GNN predicts a correction $$\delta\mathbf x_{i,k}$$ or parameter correction $$\delta\phi_{i,k}$$. The physics loss constrains the corrected trajectory rather than allowing unconstrained case predictions.

The first is more expressive; the second is often easier to interpret and less likely to violate compartment structure.

### Recommended weekly training objective

For weekly dengue data, I would use:

$$
\mathcal L
=\lambda_{\mathrm{inc}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{inc}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{flow}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{flow}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{mob}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{mob}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{mass}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{mass}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{pos}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pos}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{prior}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{prior}}.
$$

The incidence term is

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{inc}}
=\sum_{i,k}w_{i,k}
\left(\hat C_{i,k}-C_{i,k}\right)^2,
$$

or a negative-binomial likelihood for overdispersed counts. The flow residual is

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{flow}}
=\sum_{i,k}\left\|
\hat{\mathbf x}_{i,k+1}
-\Phi_{7\,\mathrm{days}}\left(\hat{\mathbf x}_{i,k},\mathbf A,\mathbf u_{i,k};\phi\right)
\right\|_{\mathbf W_x}^2.
$$

Population conservation and positivity penalties are

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{mass}}
=\sum_{i,k}\left(S_{h,i}+E_{h,i}+I_{h,i}+R_{h,i}-N_{h,i}\right)^2,
$$

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pos}}
=\sum_{i,k}\sum_m\operatorname{ReLU}(-\hat x_{i,k,m})^2.
$$

If mobility is represented as flows rather than simple adjacency, use a directed, row-normalized mobility matrix and ensure that movement conserves total population. Do not use the same graph indiscriminately for human movement and mosquito transmission: the former is spatial coupling; the latter is local host–vector infection.

## Main design choice

The cleanest implementation is a **GNN plus differentiable weekly SEIR–SEI flow map**. The GNN uses climate, mobility, history, and graph structure to predict states or time-varying parameters; the differentiable compartment solver advances the state for one week; and the mismatch between the GNN’s next-week output and the mechanistic one-week state becomes the physics loss. This preserves the mechanistic interpretation while allowing graph message passing to capture spatial spillover and unmeasured covariates.

The key caveat is terminology: SEIR-DST-style models demonstrate that compartmental dynamics can improve graph forecasts, but the retrieved record does not establish that they use an explicit PINN residual [^2]. Conversely, the dengue higher-order-network PINN explicitly applies physics-informed learning to a networked dynamical system, but it is not a standard SEIR–SEI formulation [^4].
