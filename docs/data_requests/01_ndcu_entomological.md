## Request for aggregate entomological surveillance data for an undergraduate dengue forecasting study

We are a final-year undergraduate group in the Department of Computer Science and
Engineering, University of Moratuwa, working on a research project that forecasts
district-level dengue incidence in Sri Lanka. The project is supervised by
**[ supervisor's name and title ]** and is unfunded academic work.

Our forecasting model is a compartmental SEIR–SEI model, which represents both
the human and the mosquito populations. The human side is informed by the
Epidemiology Unit's published weekly notification data. **The mosquito side
currently has no observational data behind it at all** — every vector parameter
is taken from published literature constants. Entomological surveillance would
be the first real measurement of that half of the model.

### Data requested

All aggregate. **No individual-level, household-level or patient-identifiable
data is requested.**

| Variable | Granularity | Period |
|---|---|---|
| Breteau Index | district × month | 2013 to present |
| Premise (House) Index | district × month | 2013 to present |
| Container Index | district × month | 2013 to present |
| Ovitrap positivity rate, if routinely recorded | district × month | 2013 to present |
| Number of premises inspected per survey round | district × month | 2013 to present |
| Species composition where recorded (*Aedes aegypti* / *Aedes albopictus*) | district × month | 2013 to present |

The last row matters for interpretation: the two species differ in breeding
habitat and biting behaviour, and a model that ignores the difference will
attribute the effect to the wrong cause.

**If the full series is not readily available**, any of the following would still
be valuable, in this order of preference: (a) district × quarter instead of
month; (b) a shorter period, for example 2016 onward, which covers the 2017 and
2019 epidemics; (c) province level instead of district.

### Purpose and handling

- Purpose: research only, to improve the vector component of a dengue forecasting
  model and to test whether entomological indices carry information that the case
  series does not.
- The data would be used in aggregate form for modelling and would not be
  redistributed.
- Findings will be published openly, with the National Dengue Control Unit
  credited as the source of the entomological data.
- We are happy to share our results, code and any findings relevant to the Unit's
  work before publication, and to present them at the Unit's convenience.
- Any format is workable — CSV or Excel is easiest; scanned summary tables are
  acceptable and we will transcribe them.
