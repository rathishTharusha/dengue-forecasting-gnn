\section{Models and Protocol}
\label{sec:models}

A run is a tuple (encoder, head, likelihood, features). Each element is varied
while the others are held fixed, so every difference is attributable to one
component.

\subsection{Encoders}
The five published ST-GNNs (STGAT, A3TGCN, ASTGCN, AAGCN, DCRNN) are built from
the reference implementations. The sixth encoder is the two-layer LSTM of the
SEIR-LSTM~\cite{liu2025seirlstm}. Each maps a $W=3$ week window of log cases,
plus any exogenous features, to a 64-dimensional representation $h_i$ per
district. The graph is the benchmark's district adjacency with self-loops,
row-normalised; AAGCN runs with its adaptive adjacency off, as published.

\subsection{Heads}
All heads output fold-standardised $\log(1+y)$ for $h=1,2,3$. Let $p_i$ be the
persistence anchor (last observed week) in the same space and $f(h_i)$ a
linear read-out.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{direct}: $\hat{z}_i = f(h_i)$, as in the published ST-GNNs.
\item \textbf{residual}: $\hat{z}_i = p_i + f(h_i)$.
\item \textbf{gated}: $\hat{z}_i = p_i + \sigma(a)\,(f(h_i) - p_i)$, a single
      learned gate initialised at $\sigma(-2)=0.12$. This head was added for
      \S\ref{sec:rescue}; it is the gated SEIR head with the simulator removed.
\item \textbf{SEIR decoder} (\texttt{foi}): $f(h_i)$ gives a weekly force of
      infection $\lambda_{i,h}=\exp(f(h_i)+b)$, clamped at $1/7$ per day. An
      SEIR model is stepped daily from an initial state seeded by observed
      cases: $E_0$ from cases at $t-2$ scaled by the reporting rate $\rho=1/11$,
      $S_0 = 1-0.682$ from a 2013--14 Colombo serosurvey, incubation rate
      $0.1$/day and recovery rate $1/7$/day. Predicted incidence times $\rho$
      times population gives counts. A learned scale on $E_0$ and $\rho$ is
      allowed.
\item \textbf{gated SEIR} (\texttt{foi\_res}): $\hat{z}_i = p_i +
      \sigma(a)\,(\mathrm{SEIR}(\lambda_i) - p_i)$. The simulator supplies the
      \emph{departure} from persistence. This is our SEIR-GNN when the encoder
      is a graph network, and SEIR-LSTM when it is the LSTM.
\item \textbf{metapopulation SEIR} (\texttt{foi\_meta}): the encoder predicts a
      transmission rate $\beta_i(t)$, and the simulator recomputes the force of
      infection each day as $\beta_i\sum_j C_{ij} I_j/N_j$, with
      $C=\mathrm{softmax}(\log A + \Theta)$ a learned row-stochastic coupling
      initialised at the border graph $A$, as in MepoGNN and
      CausalGNN~\cite{cao2023mepognn,wang2022causalgnn}. It is gated like
      \texttt{foi\_res}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Likelihood, features and physics penalty}
The default objective is squared error on the standardised target. The
negative-binomial (NB) alternative adds a dispersion head and minimises the NB2
negative log-likelihood with the mean $\mu = \exp(\hat z\sigma+m)-1$. Under
squared error on $\log(1+y)$, $\exp$ of the fitted value is a conditional
\emph{median}, while RMSE rewards the conditional \emph{mean}; NB's $\mu$ is the
mean. Seasonal features are $\sin$ and $\cos$ of the week of year at one and two
cycles per year. The spatial physics penalty adds
$\lambda_s\sum_{(i,j)\in E}(\hat y_i/s_i-\hat y_j/s_j)^2$ with $s_i$ the
district's training-mean cases: relative incidence should not jump across a
border.

\subsection{Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
Rolling origins at 0.55, 0.70 and 0.85 of the series, three seeds each. The 30
windows before each origin are validation, the following 15\% are test, and
normalisation uses training weeks only. Windows touching a missing week are
dropped, never imputed. Training uses Adam (learning rate $3\times10^{-3}$,
weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch 32), early stopping on validation with patience 40,
and 300--400 epochs. Each run's RMSE is pooled over its windows on raw counts,
overall and per horizon; tables report the mean over runs.

\textbf{Selection uses validation RMSE only.} Test is written to disk and
never used to choose. The protocol, and for each later experiment its arms,
control, decision rule and a prediction of the outcome, were committed before
it ran. An arm is adopted if its
mean validation delta against the control is negative, it wins at least 7 of 9
(origin, seed) units, and it does not raise horizon-3 error.

\textbf{Two pairing units.} Differences are paired on matched (origin, seed)
units and tested with an exact two-sided sign-flip permutation test, BH-adjusted
within each family. The \emph{origin} unit (seeds averaged) is what a claim about
the series rests on, but with three origins its $p$-value cannot fall below 0.25.
The (origin, seed) unit reaches 0.004 but mostly measures run-to-run stability.
Claims about the series therefore need the \textbf{nine-origin} protocol: origins
at $0.40+k/15$ ($k=0,\dots,8$), with nine disjoint test spans of 1/15 of the
series, where the origin unit reaches 0.004.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Runs are single-threaded and bit-reproducible
within one environment. Grids ran on local CPUs and on Kaggle CPU kernels that
clone a pinned commit. The two environments agree on means but not on single
runs, so every comparison pairs arms from the same grid in the same
environment.
