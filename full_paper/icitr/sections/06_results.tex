\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All numbers are RMSE in weekly cases. Development results are means over three
origins $\times$ three seeds; validation decides and test is reported beside it.

\subsection{Comparative Analysis}
\label{sec:comparative}
Table~\ref{tab:encoders} compares every encoder and baseline on identical windows.
\textbf{As published, three of five ST-GNNs fail.} Behind the direct head, AAGCN,
ASTGCN and the LSTM reach 16.71--16.91 on validation and all beat persistence (17.86),
but STGAT, A3TGCN and DCRNN reach 22.93, 28.63 and 34.64, worse than repeating last
week. They underfit rather than lack information: in their NB, seasonal-feature configuration, a
linear model fitted to their training residuals recovers 48\% and 60\% of STGAT's and
A3TGCN's validation residual variance, against 3--7\% for the working encoders.

\textbf{\model{} makes every encoder usable.} With \model{}, all six encoders score
16.57--18.35 on validation. Paired against the published head, it repairs STGAT,
A3TGCN and DCRNN by 5.95, 10.95 and 16.29 on validation and by 25.54, 23.24 and 34.50
on test, winning 9/9 runs each ($\padj=0.004$). On the working encoders it changes
validation by at most 0.14, and it lowers AAGCN's test RMSE on 9/9 runs ($-1.62$).
\textbf{With a strong encoder it beats persistence}: AAGCN with \model{} is better than
persistence on all nine runs on validation ($-1.29$) and on test ($-2.48$), and ASTGCN
on 9/9 and 8/9 ($-1.10$, $-1.26$). The repaired encoders reach persistence but do not
clearly pass it. AAGCN with \model{} has the lowest test RMSE of any configuration
(33.53), but the configuration selected on validation under the adoption rule, $B$
(AAGCN, direct head, NB loss, seasonal features), is
worse than persistence on test (37.54), so we report AAGCN with \model{} as
observed rather than selected. % EXP-062: B changes after the week-index fix.

The graph adds little: a GCN beats the same network without message passing by only
0.06 (3/9 runs), and $k$-NN analogues with no network at all reach 16.52 / 34.34.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablation}
Table~\ref{tab:rescue} builds \model{} from the published head one component at a
time. \textbf{The anchor removes the failure}: a residual head with no gate already
brings STGAT, A3TGCN and DCRNN to 17.47--17.85. \textbf{The gate} improves on it for
STGAT (17.85 to 16.98), AAGCN and ASTGCN, costs A3TGCN and DCRNN 0.21 and 0.83, and on
the prospective weeks beats the ungated head ($-0.31$, 95\% CI $[-0.77,-0.08]$).

\textbf{The SEIR branch adds nothing.} A pre-registered rule credited physics only if
the SEIR branch beat \model{} on at least 7/9 runs with $\padj<0.05$ on each failing
encoder. It does so on none: it is \emph{worse} on STGAT ($+0.66$, 1/9), ties on A3TGCN
($-0.00$, 6/9), helps DCRNN on only 3/9 runs, and costs the working encoders
0.75--0.86 (0--1/9). Routing the whole forecast through the simulator is worse still:
the pure SEIR decoder scores 24.01--25.75, worse than persistence on every encoder.
Inverting the simulator explains why. With transmission set to zero, the exposed state
seeded from recent cases already emits more cases than observed for 14--16\% of
training targets, which no network output can reach, and the required force of
infection is only $r^2=0.22$--$0.26$ predictable from case history.

\textbf{Loss.} Among more than twenty further levers, each paired against its own
control, the NB likelihood gives the largest validation gain ($-0.76$, 9/9,
$\padj=0.014$), larger than any architectural change, but it is 1.61 worse on test, so
\model{} keeps squared error. No architecture change improves validation by more than
0.15, and RevIN ($+1.47$), an STID-style MLP ($+0.84$) and TimeGAN augmentation
($+0.60$) are worse on 0/9 runs. % EXP-062: re-check the NB lever.

\subsection{Prospective Evaluation, 2024--2026}
\label{sec:prospective}
The decisive test uses weeks on which no setting was chosen. Six arms were frozen on
development data before the new weeks were parsed, and each was scored once on 85
forecast starts (Table~\ref{tab:prospective}). The neural arms share the development
finalist encoder, a Graph WaveNet-style network with a learned
adjacency~\cite{wu2019graphwavenet}.

\textbf{No learned model beats persistence on RMSE}: persistence scores 34.75 and
\model{} 35.43, a difference that is not significant ($p_{\text{Holm}}=0.62$ for the
SEIR variant). \textbf{The SEIR branch ties \model{}} ($-0.03$, $[-0.41,+0.12]$,
$p_{\text{Holm}}=0.67$). On the secondary metrics \model{} is better than persistence
(MAE 11.35 against 11.78, SMAPE 45.7 against 50.3), and every arm beats seasonal naive
by 26.7--27.7 RMSE. The 2026 outbreak dominates RMSE: persistence scores 14.72, 20.05 and
160.51 in 2024, 2025 and 2026, and \model{} 14.32, 21.43 and 162.75. \model{} is better
in the quiet year and worse in the outbreak, whose next large move is not in the inputs.
The SEIR arm's development advantage over persistence (27.34 against 28.54) does not
carry over. Because the two variants are equivalent, we present the simpler \model{};
this choice was made after the test, and the pre-registered finalist was the SEIR
variant.

\subsection{Hyperparameter Tuning and Computational Cost}
\label{sec:cost}
Every encoder and head in the main study uses the same fixed settings
(Section~\ref{sec:impl}), so no model receives more tuning than another. Before the
prospective test, each learned arm was tuned on the same grid (learning rate
$\{10^{-3},3\times10^{-3}\}$ $\times$ hidden size $\{32,64\}$, 108 runs per arm) on
validation only; all arms chose $3\times10^{-3}$ and 64, and the grid moved validation
RMSE by at most 0.57. Table~\ref{tab:compute} gives the cost. \model{} adds one parameter
and no measurable time, while the SEIR branch adds up to $2.2\times$ the time per epoch
(AAGCN) because it steps the simulator daily. The smallest encoder, AAGCN
(2{,}873 parameters, 19\,s per run), is also the most accurate.
