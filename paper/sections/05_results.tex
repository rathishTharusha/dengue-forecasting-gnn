\subsection{Comparison with the Baseline}
As shown in Table \ref{tab:main}, baseline spatio-temporal GCN (Row 1, RMSE 45.32) and GAT (Row 2, RMSE 45.50) underperform naive persistence (Row 0, RMSE 44.80) under a strict leak-free rolling-origin protocol. This empirical finding confirms that standard spatial message passing over fixed geographic graphs struggles to outperform simple temporal persistence on small regional time series ($N=25$). 

Incorporating the learned adaptive graph (Row 3, RMSE 44.85) recovers baseline performance loss by learning non-local spatial dependencies. When combined with physics-informed loss regularization, our proposed PIAG-Net (Row 5) achieves the overall lowest mean RMSE ($44.78$).

\subsection{Ablation \& Regime Analysis}
The primary strength of PIAG-Net manifests during epidemic outbreak periods (Fold 2 test weeks, origin $a=0.70$). During severe outbreak surges, standard GCN baseline degrades significantly (Fold 2 RMSE $41.93$). In contrast, PIAG-Net achieves a Fold 2 RMSE of \textbf{38.65}---a \textbf{3.28-point reduction} over baseline GCN.

Table \ref{tab:secondary} presents the hyperparameter sensitivity sweep for physics loss weight $\lambda_{\text{phys}}$. Setting $\lambda_{\text{phys}} = 0.10$ provides optimal regularization, enforcing spatial smoothness without over-constraining model capacity. Higher weights ($\lambda_{\text{phys}} = 1.0$) cause over-regularization, increasing RMSE to $46.12$.

\subsection{Forecast Behavior \& Spike Suppression}
Figure \ref{fig:forecast} illustrates weekly case predictions for Colombo district across Fold 2 test weeks. As identified by Weng et al. \cite{weng2024graph}, unconstrained spatial GNN message passing frequently generates abnormal false outbreak spikes in neighboring non-epidemic districts. As demonstrated in Figure \ref{fig:forecast}, PIAG-Net effectively eliminates false spatial spikes while accurately tracking true epidemic peaks.
